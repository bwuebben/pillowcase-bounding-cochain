\title[Exact action separation in the pillowcase]
{Exact action separation on the main component of a pretzel-tangle composition in the pillowcase}

\author{Bernd Johannes Wuebben}
\subjclass[2020]{53D12 (primary); 53D40, 57K18, 57K31 (secondary)}
\keywords{Pillowcase, traceless character variety, Lagrangian correspondence,
immersed curve, action filtration, rational tangle}
\date{August 15, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Let $P=(S^1\times S^1)/\{(\gamma,\theta)\sim(-\gamma,-\theta)\}$ be the pillowcase and let
$P^*$ be its smooth stratum.  We compose the rational-tangle arcs for $Q_{1/3}$ and $Q_{1/7}$
with the small-holonomy-perturbation correspondence of the three-strand tangle.  Smith's
cross-resolution distinguishes a main output, obtained by resolving six internal circle fibers
and continuing the branches away from them.  After a symmetric compact truncation, we prove that
the correspondence is exact and that a half-period symmetry fixes the relative primitive on the
two components of this main output.

In the singular limit the main output consists of $21$ affine branches joined across the six
circle fibers.  Their twelve endpoints have nondegenerate hyperbolic smoothing models.  The
branch-intersection equations have $58$ limiting candidates, exactly $50$ of which continue to
transverse ordered self-intersections for every sufficiently small positive perturbation.  An
area--residue calculation evaluates each limiting action as a rational multiple of $\pi^2$:
$42$ are negative and $8$ are positive, no action vanishes, and the smallest absolute limiting
action is $\pi^2/42$.  The count and all action signs persist under perturbation.  No claim is made
about auxiliary circle components allowed by the cross-resolution, the noncompact puncture ends,
holomorphic-quilt counts, bounding cochains, or the instanton--pillowcase comparison.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\section{Introduction}

The traceless $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ character variety of the four-punctured sphere is the pillowcase
\[
 P=(S^1\times S^1)/(\gamma,\theta)\sim(-\gamma,-\theta).
\]
Its smooth stratum $P^*$ is obtained by deleting the four orbifold points; we use the standard
Liouville completion near those punctures \cite[Appendix~A.1.1]{CHKK}.  Rational tangles give
proper Lagrangian arcs $A_r\subset P^*$, while a holonomy perturbation of the three-strand tangle
gives an immersed Lagrangian correspondence
\[
 C_t=C_{3,t}^{\mathrm{reg}}\iss(P_1^*)^-\times(P_2^*)^-\times P_3^*
\]
for small $t>0$ \cite{HeraldKirk,Smith}.  We first form the full direct fiber product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:composition}
 \widetilde L_t^{\mathrm{full}}=(A_{1/3}\times A_{1/7})
       \times_{P_1\times P_2} C_t,
 \qquad
 L_t^{\mathrm{full}}=\pi_3(\widetilde L_t^{\mathrm{full}})\iss P_3^*.
\end{equation}
Smith's cross-resolution theorem singles out a main subspace obtained by resolving the internal
circle fibers and continuing the off-circle branches; it also permits auxiliary circle components
that collapse toward isolated singular images as $t\to0$ \cite[Thm.~4.22]{Smith}.  Fix a generic
symmetric truncation $P^\delta\subset P^*$ containing the six circle images and all intersections
studied below.  We write
\[
 \widetilde L_t^\delta\subset \widetilde L_t^{\mathrm{full}},
 \qquad
 L_t^\delta=\pi_3(\widetilde L_t^\delta)\iss P^\delta
\]
for the compact main subspace and its output.  Auxiliary components and the portions in the
deleted end collars are not included in $L_t^\delta$.

The pair of slopes $1/3$ and $1/7$ is the character-variety composition associated to the
pretzel-tangle presentation of $P(-2,3,7)$ used in the companion calculation \cite{WuebbenII}.

The purpose of this paper is finite and geometric.  It determines the exact immersed-curve action
data of the compact main output $L_t^\delta$; it does not classify the auxiliary components of
\eqref{eq:composition} or construct any moduli space of figure-eight bubbles.
This distinction matters because strip shrinking can produce Morsified trees of several
holomorphic components \cite{BottmanWehrheim}.  A list of candidate output generators is not a
count of those trees.

Following Smith, we call the loci $\gamma=0$ and $\gamma=\pi$ in an input pillowcase the
\emph{seams}; these are not seams of a pseudoholomorphic quilt.  In the singular limit, the output
branches are either affine graph segments or images of the six circle fibers.  We call an
intersection of the two types a \emph{diagonal--circle intersection}, and an intersection of two
circle images a \emph{circle--circle intersection}.

We fix the remaining terminology.  If a transverse self-intersection of $L_t^\delta$ has two preimages
$z_-$ and $z_+$, the orientation convention for immersed Floer theory selects one ordered pair
$x=(z_-,z_+)$ in degree one.  We call this ordered generator a \emph{root}.  If $f_t$ is the
primitive inherited from the exact fiber product, its action is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:root-action-intro}
 \mathcal A_t(x)=f_t(z_-)-f_t(z_+).
\end{equation}
The word ``root'' in this paper therefore means an ordered branch jump, not a zero of an
unspecified function.  The \emph{short deck class} of a root is the reduced torus-deck
displacement of the ordinary closing loop used in the area-residue calculation; for an odd seam
separation, the construction first doubles the path and then removes that artificial factor of
two.

\begin{theorem}[Exact action theorem]\label{thm:main}
There are an equivariant Liouville completion of the pillowcase, a generic symmetric truncation
$P^\delta$, and a number $t_0>0$ with the following properties for every $0<t<t_0$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The compactly truncated correspondence $C_t^\delta$ is exact.  The main output $L_t^\delta$ inherits a
primitive whose relative normalization across its two open parameter arcs is fixed by a
half-period symmetry.
\item The compact main output $L_t^\delta$ has exactly $50$ transverse degree-one roots arising from the singular
composition: $28$ diagonal-circle roots and $22$ circle-circle roots.  They occur in $25$
half-period pairs.
\item The limiting actions as $t\to0^+$ are nonzero rational multiples of $\pi^2$.  Exactly $42$
roots have negative limiting action and $8$ have positive limiting action.  The minimum absolute
limiting action is $\pi^2/42$, and all $50$ action signs persist throughout $0<t<t_0$.
\end{enumerate}
The four positive half-period representatives have short deck classes and limiting actions
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{seam pair}&\text{short deck}&\displaystyle\lim_{t\to0^+}\mathcal A_t/\pi^2\\ \hline
(1,7)&(3,1)&3/7\\
(2,7)&(8,4)&5/14\\
(2,17)&(3,1)&3/14\\
(3,4)&(10,5)&5/42.
\end{array}
\]
\end{theorem}

The theorem concerns the main subspace $L_t^\delta$.  It neither asserts that Smith's possible
auxiliary circle components are absent from $L_t^{\mathrm{full}}$ nor assigns actions to them.

Three ingredients make the statement exact.  First, the Liouville primitive is normalized
equivariantly and its periods are checked on the full singular fiber, including seam-born and
corner-link cycles.  Hamiltonian-composition flux then transports this vanishing to $t>0$.
Second, the singular output is recorded by exact piecewise-linear first-arc data.  Its only
failures of the graph description occur at six explicitly determined stabilizer circles, whose
endpoints carry hyperbolic smoothing germs.  Third, all circle--circle intersections reduce to quadratic
equations over $\mathbb Q(2\cos(\pi/7))$.  Rational interval bounds isolate the physical solutions
and determine their derivative signs.  The action of each surviving root is then a signed area
minus a lattice-residue term.

Section~\ref{sec:exactness} fixes the primitive and proves exactness.  Section~\ref{sec:geometry}
describes the six folds, the hyperbolic smoothing, and the $21$ separated graph sheets.
Section~\ref{sec:actions} proves the exact root count and action table.
Section~\ref{sec:scope} records the precise energy consequence and the boundary of the result.

\section{Exact primitives and half-period normalization}\label{sec:exactness}

Write $(\gamma,\theta)$ for the torus-cover coordinates on the pillowcase.  On the central
cylinder
\[
 U=(0,\pi)\times S^1\subset P^*,
\]
the Goldman form is $d\gamma\wedge d\theta$.  The choice of a global primitive on the completed
four-punctured sphere includes residue data at its four ends.

\begin{lemma}[Normalized pillowcase completion]\label{lem:normalized-primitive}
Let
\[
 \tau[\gamma,\theta]=[\gamma,\theta+\pi],
 \qquad
 \sigma[\gamma,\theta]=[\pi-\gamma,-\theta].
\]
There is a $\langle\tau,\sigma\rangle$-invariant primitive $\lambda_0$ of the Goldman form on
$P^*$ such that
\[
 d\lambda_0=d\gamma\wedge d\theta,
 \qquad
 \lambda_0=(\gamma-\pi/2)\,d\theta
\]
near the circle $\{\gamma=\pi/2\}$.  Its four positively oriented puncture residues are all
$-\pi^2/2$.  For every sufficiently small invariant corner truncation
$P^\delta\subset P^*$, there is an invariant Liouville completion
$(\widehat P^\delta,\lambda)$ whose primitive agrees with $\lambda_0$ on $P^\delta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The noncompact surface $P^*$ has zero second cohomology, so its Goldman form has a primitive
$\lambda'$.  On an annular neighborhood of $\{\gamma=\pi/2\}$, add a closed one-form to kill the
period of $\lambda'-(\gamma-\pi/2)d\theta$, and then subtract the differential of a cutoff of the
resulting local function.  Average the primitive and the closed correction over
$\langle\tau,\sigma\rangle$.  Both the Goldman form and the displayed central primitive are
invariant.  The group acts transitively on the four punctures, so the four residues agree.

The torus cover has area $4\pi^2$ and is generically two-to-one over $P$, hence the Goldman area of
$P^*$ is $2\pi^2$.  Apply Stokes' theorem to an invariant exhaustion by compact surfaces.  With a
counterclockwise meridian around a deleted point declared positive, the sum of the four limiting
meridian periods is the negative of this area.  Equality of the residues therefore makes each one
$-\pi^2/2$.  The averaged closed correction does not alter them: its four residue changes are
equal and have sum zero.

Finally choose the four deleted disks equivariantly.  Outside the prescribed compact truncation,
modify the primitive in disjoint collars so that its Liouville vector field is outward transverse,
and attach the positive symplectizations of the boundary circles.  These changes can be made
equivariantly and leave $\lambda_0$ unchanged on $P^\delta$.
\end{proof}

Put
\[
 \Lambda_P=-\lambda_1-\lambda_2+\lambda_3.
\]
On a smooth torus-cover chart of the unperturbed correspondence, Smith's boundary coordinates are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smith-boundary}
 \gamma_1=\gamma_2=\gamma_3=\gamma,
 \qquad
 \theta_1=\beta\sin\alpha,
 \qquad
 \theta_2=\theta-\beta\sin\alpha,
 \qquad
 \theta_3=\theta
\end{equation}
\cite[Eqs.~(3.1.4)--(3.1.5)]{Smith}.  At $\gamma=\pi/2$, the normalized local primitive therefore
satisfies $\Lambda_P|_{C_0}=0$.

Let
\[
 \mathcal V_0=\{\sin\gamma\cos\alpha=0\},
 \qquad
 r_0=(r_1,r_2,r_3):\mathcal V_0^\delta\longrightarrow(P^\delta)^3
\]
be Smith's singular parameter fiber and its three boundary maps.  If $c$ is an oriented loop in
$\mathcal V_0^\delta$, write
\[
 h(c)=-r_{1*}[c]-r_{2*}[c]+r_{3*}[c]\in H_1(P^*;\Z).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Exactness of the small perturbation]\label{prop:perturbed-exactness}
For a generic symmetric truncation $P^\delta$ and every sufficiently small $t>0$,
\[
 [\ell_t^*\Lambda_P]=0\quad\text{in }H^1(C_t^\delta;\R),
\]
where $\ell_t:C_t^\delta\iss(P^\delta)^-\times(P^\delta)^-\times P^\delta$ is the restriction
immersion.  The assertion includes the cycles created when Smith's singular seam is smoothed and
all corner-link cycles.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We first calculate on the regular part of the singular parameter fiber.  Smith's parameter space
$\mathcal V_0^*$ is a two-fold cover of the regular correspondence and decomposes as
\[
 \mathcal V_0^*\cong
 \bigl[S^2\times(S^1\setminus S^0)\bigr]
 \sqcup
 \bigl[T^2\times(S^1\setminus S^0)\bigr]
\]
\cite[Def.~3.6]{Smith}.  Each component of the first summand has zero first real cohomology.  On a
component of the second summand, represent the two torus generators at $\gamma=\pi/2$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:smith-boundary} gives angular increments $(2\pi,-2\pi,0)$ for the
$\beta$-circle and $(0,2\pi,2\pi)$ for the $\theta$-circle.  The normalized primitive vanishes on
both generators.  The parameter involution exchanges the two components of
$S^1\setminus S^0$ and preserves the pulled-back one-form, so the period class vanishes on all of
$\mathcal V_0^*$.

It remains to include cycles through the singular seam.  Decompose the full zero set as
\[
 \mathcal V_0=A\cup H_0\cup H_\pi,
 \qquad
 A=\{\alpha=\pi/2\}\cong T^3,
 \qquad
 H_0\sqcup H_\pi=\{\gamma=0,\pi\}\cong S^0\times S^1\times S^2.
\]
and put
\[
 A_\epsilon=\{\beta=\epsilon\pi\},
 \qquad
 B_\epsilon=\{\theta-\beta=\epsilon\pi\},
 \qquad \epsilon\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Cutting $A$ along these four subtori and applying Mayer--Vietoris to the two
$S^1\times S^2$ pieces and the corner collars gives a redundant spanning set for first homology:
the two circle directions on every $A_\epsilon$ and $B_\epsilon$, together with the two directions
on every corner link.  The angular directions can be represented at $\gamma=\pi/2$ and have zero
period by the preceding paragraph.  Denote the remaining $\gamma$-directions by
$A_0^\gamma,A_\pi^\gamma,B_0^\gamma,B_\pi^\gamma$, using boundary-collar push-offs when needed.

Orient the meridians $m_{00},m_{0\pi},m_{\pi0},m_{\pi\pi}$ positively about the four deleted
points.  Their sum is zero, so use $(m_{0\pi},m_{\pi0},m_{\pi\pi})$ as a basis of
$H_1(P^*;\Z)$.  Put $\mathcal A_\delta=\int_{P^\delta}d\lambda$.  Direct substitution in the
boundary formulas, followed by Stokes' theorem on the intervening rectangles in a pillowcase
fundamental domain, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:singular-gamma-table}
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
c&h(c)\text{ in the displayed basis}&
 \displaystyle\int_c r_0^*\Lambda_P^{(00)}\\ \hline
A_0^\gamma&(0,0,0)&0\\
A_\pi^\gamma&(2,0,2)&\mathcal A_\delta\\
B_0^\gamma&(0,1,-1)&0\\
B_\pi^\gamma&(-2,-1,-1)&-\mathcal A_\delta.
\end{array}
\end{equation}
Here $\lambda^{(00)}$ is a primitive whose positively oriented meridian periods are
$(-\mathcal A_\delta,0,0,0)$, and
$\Lambda_P^{(00)}=-\lambda_1^{(00)}-\lambda_2^{(00)}+\lambda_3^{(00)}$.  The middle column records
the meridian crossed by each boundary projection as $\gamma$ passes $0$ or $\pi$; it therefore
prints every sign and multiplicity used in the comparison.

On the four boundary meridians of the symmetric truncation, the restricted primitive $\lambda$
has period $-\mathcal A_\delta/4$ by Stokes' theorem and symmetry.
Thus $\lambda-\lambda^{(00)}$ is closed and has period $-\mathcal A_\delta/4$ on each basis
meridian.  Pairing this period vector with the middle column changes the four entries in the final
column by $0,-\mathcal A_\delta,0,\mathcal A_\delta$, respectively.  All four
$\gamma$-periods therefore vanish.

Index a parameter corner by $(g,h,k)\in\{0,1\}^3$, meaning
$(\gamma,\theta,\beta)=(g\pi,h\pi,k\pi)$.  The value of $k$ does not affect the following winding
calculation.  If $(u,v)$ is an oriented basis of its torus link, the meridian winding triples in
$(P_1,P_2,P_3)$ are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corner-link-table}
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
(g,h)&(w_1,w_2,w_3)(u)&(w_1,w_2,w_3)(v)\\ \hline
(0,0)&(-1,1,0)&(1,1,2)\\
(0,1)&(-1,-1,-2)&(-1,1,0)\\
(1,0)&(1,1,2)&(-1,1,0)\\
(1,1)&(1,-1,0)&(1,1,2).
\end{array}
\end{equation}
Every vector satisfies $w_3=w_1+w_2$.  Since the four end residues agree, the signed period of a
link circle is the common residue multiplied by $-w_1-w_2+w_3$, and hence vanishes.  The
Mayer--Vietoris spanning set now shows that $r_0^*\Lambda_P$ has zero period on every class in
$H_1(\mathcal V_0^\delta;\R)$, including the classes lost when one looks only at
$\mathcal V_0^*$.

For $t>0$, cut out the perturbation solid torus.  The perturbed correspondence is a geometric
composition of a fixed restriction immersion with the solid-torus arc
\[
 [e^{is}]\longmapsto[e^{is},e^{if_t(s)}],
\]
which is a Hamiltonian deformation \cite[Eq.~(6-4) and Sec.~6B]{CHK}.  If
$v_t=\partial_t\ell_t$ is the vector field along the composed family, the Lagrangian condition gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hamiltonian-flux}
 \ell_t^*\iota_{v_t}(-\omega_1-\omega_2+\omega_3)
 =-d(H_t\circ\operatorname{pr}),
\end{equation}
with the convention $\iota_{X_{H_t}}\omega=-dH_t$.  Hence
$\partial_t(\ell_t^*\Lambda_P)=dG_t$, where
\[
 G_t=\Lambda_P(v_t)-H_t\circ\operatorname{pr}.
\]
Equivalently, $\ell^*\Lambda_P+G_t\,dt$ is closed on the smooth trace of the family.

Let $q_t:\mathcal V_t^\delta\to C_t^\delta$ be Smith's two-fold parameter cover and let
$\mathfrak V^\delta$ be the closure of its compact trace over $0\le t\le\epsilon$.  Smith's
explicit equation and analytic boundary maps make
$(\mathfrak V^\delta,\mathcal V_0^\delta)$ a compact subanalytic pair, hence a compatibly
triangulable pair.  A regular neighborhood $N$ of the zero-fiber subcomplex retracts onto
$\mathcal V_0^\delta$.  After decreasing $\epsilon$, the entire trace lies in $N$: otherwise a
sequence outside $N$ with $t\to0$ would limit to a point of
$\mathcal V_0^\delta\subset\operatorname{int}N$.  Consequently every one-cycle
$\widetilde a_t$ in a positive fiber is joined inside the total trace by a singular two-chain to
a cycle $a_0$ in the full zero set; a vanishing cycle is the case $a_0=0$.

The Hamiltonian graph and all boundary maps extend analytically to $t=0$, so the pulled-back trace
form extends continuously and is smooth on every simplex stratum.  Stokes' theorem applied
stratum by stratum gives
\[
 \int_{\widetilde a_t}q_t^*\ell_t^*\Lambda_P
 =\int_{a_0}r_0^*\Lambda_P=0.
\]
Thus $q_t^*[\ell_t^*\Lambda_P]=0$.  Pullback on first real cohomology under a finite cover is
injective by averaging over the deck group, which proves the proposition.
\end{proof}

The rational arcs are contractible and can be normalized equivariantly, so
$A_{1/3}\times A_{1/7}$ is exact.  Primitives add under transverse geometric composition.
Proposition~\ref{prop:perturbed-exactness} therefore gives a primitive on every component of the
full direct fiber product \eqref{eq:composition}, and hence on its compact main subspace.  The main
output, however, passes through two deleted
corners in each lifted period.  Its two open arcs initially have independent additive constants;
the next proposition fixes their relative normalization.

\begin{proposition}[Half-period normalization]\label{prop:half-period}
On the parameter cover of the direct composition, all-corner points on the main lift are
\[
 z_j=(\gamma,\theta,\alpha,\beta)
     =(21j\pi,10j\pi,\pi/2,7j\pi),
 \qquad j\in\Z.
\]
Removing two consecutive corner points cuts a lifted period into two open arcs.  Define
\[
 T(\gamma,\theta,\alpha,\beta)
  =(\gamma+21\pi,\theta+10\pi,\alpha,\beta+7\pi).
\]
Modulo the parameter period lattice, $T$ is an involution exchanging the two arcs.  With
$\rho=\tau\sigma$, the boundary maps satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:half-period-boundary}
 r_1T=\rho r_1,
 \qquad r_2T=\rho r_2,
 \qquad r_3T=\sigma r_3.
\end{equation}
The inherited primitive $F$ may be normalized so that $F\circ T=F$.

Let $I_0$ be the open arc $0<\gamma<21\pi$, let $I_1=T(I_0)$, and define
\[
 \operatorname{can}(z)=z\quad(z\in I_0),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{can}(z)=T^{-1}z\quad(z\in I_1).
\]
For every root $x=(z_-,z_+)$, including a root whose preimages lie on different open arcs,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-action}
 \mathcal A_t(x)
 =\int_{\operatorname{can}(z_+)}^{\operatorname{can}(z_-)}r_3^*\lambda,
\end{equation}
where the integral follows the oriented subarc of $I_0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At a parameter corner $(g,h,k)$, the three boundary characters are
$(g,k)$, $(g,k+h)$, and $(g,h)$.  For odd $q$, a corner $(g,r)$ lies on $A_{1/q}$ precisely when
$r=g$.  The two input equations therefore give $k=g$ and $h=0$, which yields exactly the displayed
corner lifts.  Their consecutive spacing shows that each full period contains two corner cuts.

The square of $T$ is the parameter period $(42\pi,20\pi,0,14\pi)$.  Direct substitution in the
defining traceless equation shows that $T$ preserves the parameter space, and substitution in the
boundary words gives \eqref{eq:half-period-boundary}.  The map $\rho$ preserves both odd-slope
arcs.  Average the primitive on each rational arc under $\rho$ and the primitive on $C_t$ under
$T$.  The normalized Liouville form is invariant under $\tau$ and $\sigma$, so the averages have
the same exterior derivatives and the composition primitive satisfies $F\circ T=F$.

On either open component, $dF=r_3^*\lambda$.  Replace both endpoints by their representatives in
$I_0$ and integrate from the positive endpoint to the negative endpoint.  The interior of $I_0$
contains no all-corner point, so no residue detour is involved and
\eqref{eq:canonical-action} follows.
\end{proof}

\section{The six folds and the separated graph sheets}\label{sec:geometry}

The main part of the partial composition
\[
 F_t^{\mathrm{main}}=A_{1/3}\times_{P_1}C_t\iss P_2^-\times P_3
\]
is used here only to describe the finite geometry of the direct output.  No associativity or
curved-composition theorem is invoked.

\begin{proposition}[Six-circle and fold table]\label{prop:six-fold-table}
The singular main partial composition has six stabilizer circles.  Their $P_2$ seam values, the
corresponding $A_{1/3}$ angle $\theta_1$, and the image interval $[\ell_i,h_i]$ in the third
pillowcase are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
i&(\gamma_2,\theta_2)&\theta_1&[\ell_i,h_i]\\ \hline
1&(0,2\pi/7)&2\pi/3&[8\pi/21,20\pi/21]\\
2&(0,4\pi/7)&2\pi/3&[2\pi/21,16\pi/21]\\
3&(0,6\pi/7)&2\pi/3&[4\pi/21,10\pi/21]\\
4&(\pi,\pi/7)&\pi/3&[4\pi/21,10\pi/21]\\
5&(\pi,3\pi/7)&\pi/3&[2\pi/21,16\pi/21]\\
6&(\pi,5\pi/7)&\pi/3&[8\pi/21,20\pi/21].
\end{array}
\]
If $\psi$ parametrizes a stabilizer circle, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:circle-fold}
 \cos\theta_3(\psi)
 =\cos\theta_1\cos\theta_2
  +\sin\theta_1\sin\theta_2\cos\psi.
\end{equation}
Both endpoints of every interval are nondegenerate folds.  The pillowcase symmetry pairs rows
$(1,6)$, $(2,5)$, and $(3,4)$, including their fold coefficients.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A lift of $A_{1/q}$ satisfies $q\theta-\gamma\in2\pi\Z$.  Its noncorner seam values are
\[
 A_{1/3}:\ (0,2\pi/3),\ (\pi,\pi/3)
\]
and
\[
 A_{1/7}:\ (0,2\pi k/7),\ (\pi,(2k-1)\pi/7),
 \qquad k=1,2,3.
\]
Pairing the unique $A_{1/3}$ value on each seam with the three $A_{1/7}$ values gives the six
circles.  Choose the common seam axis as polar axis.  The Euclidean dot product of the two
remaining axes gives \eqref{eq:circle-fold}, and hence
\[
 [\ell_i,h_i]
 =\bigl[|\theta_1-\theta_2|,
         \pi-|\pi-(\theta_1+\theta_2)|\bigr].
\]
At the two endpoints,
\begin{align*}
 \theta_3(\psi)&=\ell_i+
 \frac{\sin\theta_1\sin\theta_2}{2\sin\ell_i}\psi^2+O(\psi^4),\\
 \theta_3(\pi+u)&=h_i-
 \frac{\sin\theta_1\sin\theta_2}{2\sin h_i}u^2+O(u^4).
\end{align*}
All coefficients are positive and finite.  The symmetry assertion follows by replacing the two
input angles by their complements.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Hyperbolic smoothing at the twelve endpoints]
\label{prop:hyperbolic-normal-form}
Use the group-presentation convention
\[
 p=\exp\!\bigl(t\,\operatorname{Im}(ac^{-1}x)\bigr),
 \qquad
 \Phi_t=\operatorname{Re}(xp^{-1}a^{-1}pb),
\]
with
\[
 a=\mathbf i,
 \quad b=e^{\gamma\mathbf k}\mathbf i,
 \quad c=e^{\theta\mathbf k}\mathbf i,
 \quad
 x=\sin\alpha\cos\beta\,\mathbf i
   +\sin\alpha\sin\beta\,\mathbf j+\cos\alpha\,\mathbf k.
\]
Near the lower and upper endpoints of the $i$th circle there are smooth coordinates $(u,v)$ in
which
\[
 \{\Phi_t=0\}=\{uv=\tau_i^\pm(t)\},
 \qquad
 \tau_i^\pm(0)=0,
 \qquad
 (\tau_i^\pm)'(0)=\mp2\sin\theta_1\sin\theta_2.
\]
Thus all twelve endpoints have nondegenerate hyperbolic smoothing germs, with opposite sectors at
the two ends of each circle and the same three symmetry pairs as above.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At $t=0$ the defining equation is
\[
 \Phi_0(\gamma,\theta,\alpha,\beta)=\sin\gamma\cos\alpha.
\]
Write $X=\gamma-\gamma_i$ and $Y=\alpha-\pi/2$, where
$\gamma_i\in\{0,\pi\}$ and $\epsilon_i=\cos\gamma_i$.  Quaternion multiplication gives
\[
 \Phi_t
 =\epsilon_i\bigl(-XY-2t\sin\beta\sin(\theta-\beta)\bigr)
  +O\!\left(t(|X|+|Y|)+t^2+(|X|+|Y|)^3\right).
\]
The Hessian in $(X,Y)$ is nonsingular with index one.  The parametric Morse lemma therefore gives
$uv=\tau(t)$ and
\[
 \tau'(0)=-2\sin\beta\sin(\theta-\beta).
\]
At the lower and upper lifts the products are respectively
$-\sin\theta_1\sin\theta_2$ and $\sin\theta_1\sin\theta_2$, proving the formulas.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Off-seam graph sheets]\label{prop:graph-sheets}
Away from neighborhoods of the six seam values, every branch of $F_0^{\mathrm{main}}$ is locally the graph of the
symplectic shear
\[
 \phi_c(\gamma,\eta)=(\gamma,\eta+\gamma/3+c).
\]
On a torus-cover chart, the $21$ local lifts of the direct composition are parallel and pairwise
disjoint.  Both statements persist on compact off-seam subsets for all sufficiently small $t>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the unperturbed main stratum, the three boundary coordinates are
\[
 p_1=(\gamma,\beta),
 \qquad p_2=(\gamma,\theta-\beta),
 \qquad p_3=(\gamma,\theta).
\]
The first rational arc gives $\beta=\gamma/3+c$.  With $\eta=\theta-\beta$, this is the displayed
shear, and
$d\gamma\wedge d(\eta+\gamma/3+c)=d\gamma\wedge d\eta$.
By Smith's cross-resolution theorem, the main subspace is obtained from these graph branches by
replacing neighborhoods of the six internal circle fibers with their local resolutions
\cite[Thm.~4.22]{Smith}.  Thus every failure of the graph description within the main subspace is
contained in those six neighborhoods.  The auxiliary circles allowed by Smith's theorem are
separate components of the full fiber product and are not part of
$F_t^{\mathrm{main}}$.

Choose lifts
\[
 \beta=\frac{\gamma+2\pi k}{3},
 \qquad
 \eta=\frac{\gamma+2\pi\ell}{7},
 \qquad (k,\ell)\in\Z/3\times\Z/7.
\]
Then
\[
 \theta=\frac{10}{21}\gamma+
 \frac{2\pi(7k+3\ell)}{21}.
\]
The homomorphism $(k,\ell)\mapsto7k+3\ell$ from
$\Z/3\times\Z/7$ to $\Z/21$ is bijective, so the $21$ lifts have distinct intercepts.  A compact
off-seam set has positive minimum separation, which persists under the $C^1$ small-$t$
deformation.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Algebraic normal roots]\label{lem:normal-roots}
Put $x=\cos\theta$ and $u=2\cos(\pi/7)$, so
\[

\]
On row $i$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:six-fold-table}, the small-$t$ normal displacement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:normal-displacement}

\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-square}
 \cos^2\alpha_i(\theta)
 =\frac{b_i^2-(\cos\theta-a_i)^2}{\sin^2\theta},
 \qquad

 \begin{cases}+1,&i=1,2,3,\\-1,&i=4,5,6,
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
and
\[
\begin{gathered}
 a_1=a_6=\frac{2-u^2}{4},
 \qquad
 a_2=a_5=\frac{u^2-u-1}{4},
 \qquad

 b_i^2=\frac34(1-4a_i^2).
\end{gathered}
\]
The physical simple solutions of $G_i=G_j$ on $0<\theta<\pi$ are exactly
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}

$\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{4,6\},\{5,6\}$.

\begin{proof}
At $t=0$, substitution of the seam rational-arc equations into the quaternionic boundary words
gives \eqref{eq:alpha-square}.  Differentiating the defining equation in the normal $\gamma$
direction and in $t$ gives

 \partial_\gamma\Phi_0=(-1)^j\cos\alpha_i,
 \qquad

\]
which proves \eqref{eq:normal-displacement}.  The formulas for $a_i$ and $b_i$ follow from the
minimal polynomial of $u$.

denominators gives the three quadratics $P_{rs}(x)=A_{rs}x^2+B_{rs}x+C_{rs}$ with
\begin{align*}

 &=\left(u^2-\frac12u-\frac32,
   -\frac34u^2-\frac12u+2,
   \frac14u^2-\frac18u-\frac38\right),\\
 (A_{23},B_{23},C_{23})
 &=\left(u^2+u-2,
   u^2-\frac52u-2,

\end{align*}
The rational interval

\]

gives one physical root of $P_{12}$, one of $P_{13}$, and both roots of $P_{23}$ in the displayed

\begin{lemma}[Area--residue identity on the torus cover]\label{lem:area-residue}
Use scaled universal-cover coordinates
\[
 x=\gamma/\pi,\qquad y=\theta/\pi.
\]
Write $\widetilde\lambda_0$ for the lift of the normalized primitive from
Lemma~\ref{lem:normalized-primitive}.  The deleted orbifold points lift to $\Z^2$, the lifted
symplectic form is $\pi^2\,dx\wedge dy$, and $\widetilde\lambda_0$ has limiting period
$-\pi^2$ around every positively oriented small meridian of a point in $\Z^2$.  Hence every
piecewise smooth closed curve $\Gamma\subset\R^2\setminus\Z^2$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed-area-residue}
 \frac1{\pi^2}\int_\Gamma\widetilde\lambda_0
 =\operatorname{Area}(\Gamma)
  -\sum_{z\in\Z^2}\operatorname{wind}_z(\Gamma),
\end{equation}
where
$\operatorname{Area}(\Gamma)=\int_{\R^2}\operatorname{wind}_q(\Gamma)\,dq$ is signed area.

Suppose a path segment has deck-related endpoints.  Concatenate it with its image under the
pillowcase involution and join the remaining endpoints by an involution-paired choice of closing
arcs.  If $a$ and $w$ denote the signed area and total lattice winding of the resulting closed
curve, then the original segment has integral $\pi^2(a-w)/2$.  If the segment must first be
doubled to obtain deck-related endpoints, its integral is $\pi^2(a-w)/4$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The torus cover has Goldman area $4\pi^2$ and is generically two-to-one over the pillowcase, so the
pillowcase area is $2\pi^2$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:normalized-primitive}, the four positively
oriented meridian periods are equal.  Their sum is the negative of the total area by Stokes'
theorem, so each is $-\pi^2/2$.  A small meridian about a lattice point in the torus cover maps
with degree two to a pillowcase meridian; its lifted period is therefore $-\pi^2$.

Apply Stokes' theorem to the winding-number two-chain bounded by $\Gamma$, with small disks about
the lattice points removed.  The area term is $\pi^2\operatorname{Area}(\Gamma)$, and a puncture
with winding $m$ contributes $m\pi^2$ from the clockwise boundary of its deleted disk.  Moving
these boundary terms to the other side gives \eqref{eq:closed-area-residue}.

The normalized primitive is invariant under the pillowcase involution.  The two closing arcs can
be chosen as involution images with opposite orientations, so their integrals cancel, while the
original segment and its involuted image have equal integrals.  The closed curve therefore has
twice the integral of the segment.  If an initial deck-doubling was required, the doubled segment
has twice the original integral once more.  This proves both factors.
\end{proof}

normal solutions from Lemma~\ref{lem:normal-roots} leaves one simple root in $22$ and none in the eight

neighborhoods in the compact main domain, Proposition~\ref{prop:graph-sheets} gives a positive
minimum separation between every pair of output branches.  This separation persists under the
$C^1$ small-$t$ deformation.  In a fold neighborhood,
Proposition~\ref{prop:hyperbolic-normal-form} identifies the entire local main resolution with one
embedded hyperbola.  The implicit-function theorem continues each of the $50$ simple roots
uniquely.  These sets cover the compact main domain, so there are no additional roots there.

 p=(j,h/21),

evaluated at a rational midpoint even when the actual normal root is algebraic.

$3\pi^2/7$.  The pair $(3,4)$ has
$(a,w,\kappa)=(-116/21,-6,1/2)$, so the $4\to3$ action is $5\pi^2/42$.  The half-period map sends
\[
 (j,k,d)\longmapsto(21-k,21-j,d)

and preserves the normalized action, ordering, and short deck class.  Each row therefore has
multiplicity two.  Reading the last column proves the sign count and the minimum gap.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Small-perturbation persistence]\label{cor:persistence}
There is $t_0>0$ such that, for every $0<t<t_0$, the compact main output $L_t^\delta$ has exactly the $50$ roots in
Proposition~\ref{prop:action-table}, and all have the signs shown there.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Transversality continues all $50$ roots uniquely.  Compact separation of the graph sheets and the
fold neighborhoods excludes new roots.  Proposition~\ref{prop:half-period} gives primitives that
vary continuously with $t$, and the canonical paths remain in the compact corner-free arc $I_0$.
Thus every action converges to its table value.  Since the finite minimum absolute limiting value
is $\pi^2/42$, every sign is constant after decreasing $t_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Exactness is Proposition~\ref{prop:perturbed-exactness}, and the relative primitive is fixed by
Proposition~\ref{prop:half-period}.  Proposition~\ref{prop:action-table} proves the complete root
and limiting-action count.  Corollary~\ref{cor:persistence} proves persistence for small positive
$t$.
\end{proof}

\section{Energy interpretation and scope}\label{sec:scope}

The sign table has an elementary consequence for any exact holomorphic configuration that may be
defined using $L_t^\delta$.  The statement does not assume that a compactified figure-eight operation
exists.

\begin{lemma}[Root-action identity]\label{lem:energy-action}
Let a finite tree of exact pseudoholomorphic or Morse components have no external inputs and one
external root $x$ on $L_t^\delta$.  Suppose its seam and boundary lifts are fixed and every component has
the usual Stokes energy identity.  Then
\[
 E_{\mathrm{tot}}=\mathcal A_t(x).
\]
Consequently, a nonconstant such configuration can be rooted only at a positive-action root.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply Stokes' theorem to each component.  At every internal flag the same primitive difference
appears with opposite signs on the two adjacent components.  The action drops along Morse edges
cancel in the same way.  With no external inputs, only the external output remains.  Compatible
almost-complex and Morse data make each energy summand nonnegative, and a nonconstant component
makes the total positive.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:eight-candidates}
For $0<t<t_0$, every nonconstant exact zero-input configuration satisfying the hypotheses of
Lemma~\ref{lem:energy-action} is rooted at one of the eight positive roots in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[No counting conclusion]\label{rem:no-count}
Corollary~\ref{cor:eight-candidates} is an action obstruction, not an existence or parity theorem.
It does not assert that any of the eight positive roots supports a holomorphic configuration, that
only one vertex occurs, or that a sum of outputs defines a bounding cochain.  Those conclusions
would require compactification, transversality, gluing, local mod-two counts, and continuation
data that are not constructed here.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Reproducibility]\label{rem:reproducibility}
In the arXiv source archive, the command
\[
 \texttt{python3 anc/q7\_exact\_actions.py}
\]
reconstructs the singular first-arc data, verifies the rational root enclosures and derivative
signs, enumerates all $58$ overlaps, and prints the $25$-row area--residue table using exact
rational arithmetic.  The standard-library program reproduces the finite calculations; the proof
does not depend on its execution.
\end{remark}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} therefore supplies the complete finite action filtration of the compact
main output.  It does not extend that filtration to auxiliary components or the deleted end
collars.  Holomorphic and categorical questions remain outside the claimed result.
